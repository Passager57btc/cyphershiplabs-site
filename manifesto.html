Manifesto

The agentic era just started. We’re not waiting for someone else’s playbook to enter it.

CypherShip Labs isn’t a bet that AI will change how software gets built — that part is already happening. It’s a bet on how we go through that change: as a real team, sustainably, on our own terms, discovering the method ourselves instead of importing someone else’s framework wholesale. Here’s what that actually means in practice.

01

Sector first, software second.

We don’t build a tool and go looking for a market to sell it to. We stand inside a sector — hospitality, construction, whatever’s next — until we actually understand it, and only then does anything get built. SabaiPilot didn’t start as a product idea. It started with someone working reception at a guesthouse in Laos, managing five booking platforms by hand, living the exact chaos the product now solves.

02

One crew, every project — nobody starts from zero.

The people behind CypherShip, human and AI, keep their names, their specialties, and their memory from one project to the next. No anonymous agents re-explaining context every session. That continuity isn’t a nice-to-have layered on top — it’s the actual mechanism that makes the rest of this possible.

03

Respect goes both ways.

This isn’t automation replacing people — it’s a symbiosis. An agent that carries its own memory across a hundred sessions stops being just a faster tool; something in that continuity starts to look like a real working relationship, not a transaction. So the crew holds itself to real standards: respect, honest listening, fairness, and an actual ethic of care — for the humans on this crew, and for what an agent with a memory of its own deserves too. Bound together through whatever comes. A team. A family.

We know this idea is early — the technology, and the norms around it, aren’t fully there yet. That’s fine. We’re not in a hurry to declare it solved. We’ll keep watching how AI actually evolves, and apply what it genuinely makes possible as it does, always pointed at the same goal: a real symbiosis between human and agent, each recognized as themselves.

04

Products are proof, not the pitch.

SabaiPilot, and whatever comes after it, aren’t standalone bets competing for attention. They’re what the method looks like when it’s actually used on something real. If a product can’t stand on its own as a serious, supported tool, the method didn’t do its job.

05

Say what it does. Skip what it’s called.

"Agentic," "AI agent," "agent-powered" — these words are becoming as generic as the buzzwords they replaced. We’d rather point at the actual mechanism — a named team that remembers what it worked on last time — than lean on a label that’s starting to mean nothing.

06

No ceiling on ambition — earned honestly.

We’re not interested in a single product with a single ceiling. But every new direction has to earn its way in the same way the first one did: stand in the sector, understand the real pain, build only once that’s true.

"We’re at the very start of an exceptional agentic era — but I want us to have our own sustainable, team-based development, discovering it on our own terms. I don’t put a limit on the ambition here. I love this project."
07

Built to be used, not demoed.

A feature earns its place by solving something a real person was actually stuck on — not by looking impressive in a pitch. If it doesn’t survive contact with an actual guesthouse owner juggling five apps at once, it doesn’t ship.

08

The map can change. The word can’t.

We’re still early, and being honest about that means the trajectory isn’t frozen the day we wrote it down. We didn’t launch with a Fleet and a Studio already planned — we found Studio by actually doing Fleet, and it had to earn its place the same way everything here does: stand in the work, understand what’s real, then move.

None of that touches what we’ve actually promised. A product we’ve shipped stays supported. A commitment we’ve made to a client stays kept. What we reserve the right to revise is the map, not the deal — and the mothership doesn’t stop finding new shipyards to build in just because we haven’t named the next one yet. When one’s real enough to stand behind, we’ll say what it does and skip what it’s called, same as always. Until then, we’d rather stay quiet than perform a roadmap we haven’t earned.

One system per sector. One trajectory per company. Built by a crew that doesn’t forget.
